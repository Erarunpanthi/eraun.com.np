Irrigation

Duty of Water, Delta and Base Period

PSC Subjective Note · Nepal Engineering Service (Civil), Second Paper

i. Duty of Water

Duty of water is the relationship between the volume of water supplied and the area of land it matures. It is the area of land, in hectares, irrigated for the complete growth of a crop by a continuous supply of one cumec of water throughout the crop’s base period.

Duty is generally expressed in hectares per cumec (ha/cumec) and is denoted by D.

D = A / Q

Where A is the irrigated area in hectares and Q is the discharge in cumecs.

ii. Delta

Each crop requires a certain amount of water at intervals throughout its growth period. The depth applied at each irrigation may vary (often about 5–10 cm) depending on the crop, climate and soil. The interval between two consecutive waterings is called the irrigation frequency or rotation period.

Delta (Δ) is the total depth of irrigation water required by a crop over its entire maturation period, or base period. It is usually expressed in centimetres; use metres in the duty–delta relationship below unless the centimetre form is specified.

iii. Base Period

The base period (B) is the time between the first watering of a crop, around sowing or planting, and its last watering before harvesting. It is usually measured in days.

Relationship between Duty, Delta and Base Period

Consider a crop with a base period of B days. If one cumec of water is supplied continuously during this period, the supplied volume is:

V = Q × time = 1 × 86,400 × B = 86,400B m³

Since one hectare equals 10,000 m², the volume applied over D hectares at a depth of Δ metres is:

V = D × 10,000 × Δ = 10,000DΔ m³

Equating the two volumes:

86,400B = 10,000DΔ   ⇒   D = (8.64 × B) / Δ

In this form, Δ is in metres and B is in days. If delta is entered in centimetres, use the equivalent form:

D = (864 × B) / Δcm
