01FOUNDATIONS

Engineering fundamentals

Revisit the principles behind common civil engineering calculations and materials.

  • Engineering mechanics & strength of materials
  • Construction materials and building construction
  • Engineering drawing and basic mechanics
02FIELD METHODS

Surveying & geomatics

Connect field procedures with calculations, checks, and clear recording.

  • Levelling, traversing & coordinate work
  • Contours, areas, volumes & curves
  • Survey instruments, errors & adjustments
03GROUND ENGINEERING

Geology, soil & foundations

Build a sound understanding of ground conditions and how they affect design.

  • Engineering geology and soil properties
  • Compaction, seepage & effective stress
  • Foundation types and basic behaviour
04STRUCTURES

Structural engineering

Review analysis concepts and the behaviour of common structural systems.

  • Structural analysis and reinforced concrete
  • Steel, timber & masonry structures
  • Loads, stability and detailing concepts
05WATER SYSTEMS

Hydraulics, hydrology & irrigation

Practise flow relationships and review water-resource systems and works.

  • Fluid mechanics and open-channel flow
  • Hydrology, irrigation & drainage
  • Water supply and basic hydraulic structures
06TRANSPORT

Transportation engineering

Study how routes, pavements, traffic, and transport facilities are planned.

  • Highway alignment and geometric design
  • Pavements, materials & traffic engineering
  • Construction and maintenance concepts
07ENVIRONMENT & PUBLIC HEALTH

Environmental engineering

Reinforce the civil engineering systems that protect public health.

  • Water treatment and quality basics
  • Sanitation, wastewater & solid waste
  • Environmental safeguards and impacts
08COST & QUANTITIES

Estimation & valuation

Practise systematic measurement, quantity take-off, and cost reasoning.

  • Measurement rules and quantity calculations
  • Rate analysis and bill preparation
  • Valuation and cost-estimate fundamentals
09DELIVERY & PRACTICE

Project & professional practice

Connect technical work with responsible delivery and the requirements in your notice.

  • Construction management, planning & quality
  • Contracts, procurement & site safety
  • Ethics, public service or legal topics where listed

QUICK REVISION PROMPTS

Recall the idea before opening your notes

Tap to reveal

Use each card as a short retrieval-practice prompt. These reminders are introductory; check assumptions, units, and methods in your prescribed references.

01What relates discharge, area, and mean velocity?Fluid mechanics · Continuity+

Q = A × V

For a section with mean velocity V and flow area A, discharge Q is area multiplied by mean velocity.

02How do you find a foresight RL from one setup?Surveying · Height of instrument+

RL = known RL + BS − FS

First find the height of instrument (known RL + backsight), then subtract the foresight.

03What is gravimetric water content?Soil mechanics · Index properties+

w = (mass of water ÷ mass of dry soil) × 100%

Keep the mass basis clear. If water content is used as a decimal in a calculation, convert from percent first.

04What is the peak moment for a full-span UDL on a simple beam?Structures · Beam actions+

Mmax = wL² ÷ 8

For a simply supported beam of span L carrying a uniformly distributed load w over the full span.

05What does a water–cement ratio compare?Construction materials · Concrete+

w/c = mass of water ÷ mass of cement

It is a mass ratio. In practice, concrete quality also depends on materials, proportioning, placement, and curing.

06Under uniform open-channel flow, how do bed and energy slopes relate?Hydraulics · Manning equation+

Senergy = Sbed

This equality is used for uniform flow. Confirm the assumptions before applying Manning’s equation.
